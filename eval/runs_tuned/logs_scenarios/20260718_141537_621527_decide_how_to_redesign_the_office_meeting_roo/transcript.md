# Decide how to redesign the office meeting-room booking policy

The office meeting-room booking policy needs redesign to improve fairness and efficiency. Six participants will decide on a policy that balances booking flexibility, room utilization, and ease of use for all employees.

## Options

- A) First-Come, First-Served Booking System — booking method: online portal; max booking duration hours: 2; advance booking limit days: 14; cancellation policy: allowed up to 1 hour before (+ Simple and transparent process encourages quick reservations; − High demand may cause popular slots to fill quickly, limiting access)
- B) Priority-Based Booking with Department Quotas — booking method: online portal with department login; max booking duration hours: 3; department quota per week: 5; priority levels: senior staff have booking priority (+ Ensures fair room access across departments and seniority levels; − Complexity may slow down booking and require administrative oversight)
- C) Time-Block Allocation with Rotating Schedule — booking method: weekly schedule published in advance; time block length minutes: 60; rotation frequency: weekly rotation among teams; max bookings per user per week: 2 (+ Equitable distribution of meeting times reduces booking conflicts; − Less flexibility for spontaneous meetings and urgent needs)
- D) On-Demand Booking with Usage Tracking and Limits — booking method: mobile app with real-time availability; max booking duration hours: 1.5; weekly usage limit hours: 6; usage reporting: weekly summary sent to users (+ Encourages efficient room use and self-monitoring by users; − Requires users to monitor their usage actively to avoid limits)
