# Select a meal-prep approach for busy exam weeks

During busy exam weeks, preparing meals efficiently helps maintain energy and focus. The group must choose a meal-prep approach that balances time, nutrition, and ease of preparation for two participants.

## Options

- A) Batch Cooking on Weekends — prep time per week: 3-4 hours; meals prepared: 10-14 meals; nutritional balance: moderate; storage required: large fridge/freezer space (+ Reduces daily cooking time during exam days; − Requires significant upfront time and storage space)
- B) Daily Quick Assembly Meals — prep time per day: 15-20 minutes; meals prepared: 1 meal per day; nutritional balance: variable; ingredients complexity: low (+ Keeps meals fresh and adaptable to daily preferences; − Daily prep can add up and interrupt study time)
- C) Pre-portioned Ingredient Kits — prep time per meal: 20-30 minutes; meals prepared: 5-7 meals; nutritional balance: high; cost per meal: moderate (+ Simplifies cooking with ready-to-use ingredients; − Requires some cooking skills and planning)
- D) Ready-to-Eat Meal Delivery — prep time: 0 minutes; meals per week: 7-14 meals; nutritional balance: varies by provider; cost per meal: higher (+ Saves time with no cooking required; − Higher cost and less control over ingredients)
