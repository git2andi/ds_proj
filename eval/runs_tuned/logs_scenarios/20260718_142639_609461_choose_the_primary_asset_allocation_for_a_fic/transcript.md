# Choose the primary asset allocation for a fictional investment club portfolio

An investment club with moderate risk tolerance is selecting a primary asset allocation for a new portfolio. The portfolio aims for balanced growth and income over a 5-10 year horizon with an initial capital of $100,000.

## Options

- A) 60% Stocks, 30% Bonds, 10% Cash Allocation — equity exposure: 60%; fixed income exposure: 30%; liquidity: 10%; expected return range: 5-7% annually; risk level: moderate (+ Balances growth potential with income and liquidity; − May underperform in strong equity bull markets)
- B) 80% Stocks, 15% Bonds, 5% Cash Allocation — equity exposure: 80%; fixed income exposure: 15%; liquidity: 5%; expected return range: 7-9% annually; risk level: moderately high (+ Higher growth potential from increased equity exposure; − Greater volatility and potential for short-term losses)
- C) 40% Stocks, 50% Bonds, 10% Cash Allocation — equity exposure: 40%; fixed income exposure: 50%; liquidity: 10%; expected return range: 3-5% annually; risk level: moderate to low (+ More stable income with reduced portfolio volatility; − Lower growth potential may not meet long-term goals)
- D) 100% Stocks Allocation — equity exposure: 100%; fixed income exposure: 0%; liquidity: 0%; expected return range: 8-10% annually; risk level: high (+ Maximizes growth potential over the investment horizon; − High volatility and risk of significant short-term losses)
